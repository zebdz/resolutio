Your vote. Your evidence.

Know what happened
to your vote.

Keep a copy of the vote you submitted. Compare it with the final voting record. Keep independent evidence that makes later changes detectable.

A successful receipt check shows that your saved ballot is included unchanged in the sealed voting record. The seal and a confirmed timestamp then let that record be checked for later changes.

Three things make this possible

  1. Your original receipt

    After submitting your vote, check that the receipt shows your intended answers. Save the original receipt file, either downloaded or attached to your receipt email.

    It gives you a copy to compare with the final record.

  2. A sealed voting record

    After voting closes, the recorded votes and results are sealed together. They receive a mathematical “fingerprint” that changes if their contents change.

    Changes can be detected by comparing against the saved proof.

  3. An independent timestamp

    A confirmed timestamp records the fingerprint using Bitcoin. It provides evidence that the sealed record existed by that time.

    You can keep the proof and check it independently of our website.

What if “Yes” becomes “No”, or a choice is missing?

The original-receipt check cannot pass. A changed, missing or extra choice in the supplied ballot will not match your saved receipt. Keep the original file and the verification result so the issue can be investigated.

You can check the totals, too

In a non-anonymous poll, participants with access to the full voting record can verify the count using the sealed vote records. Other voters’ original receipts and secret codes remain private.

  1. Download the voting record. It contains the questions and answers, participant identities, recorded choices and voting weights, published totals, and verification evidence.
  2. Check the files with an independent verifier. It checks that the downloaded votes and results match the sealed fingerprint. Timestamp confirmation is checked separately.
  3. Recount and compare. The verifier counts the selections and adds their voting weights for each answer, then compares those calculations with the published totals. You can also inspect the vote entries yourself.

This establishes that the totals match the votes in the sealed record. Comparing a voter’s original receipt with that record is the separate check that their ballot matches the one they saved when voting.

How can a vote be checked without its private receipt?

Each shared vote record includes the individual random value (called a “salt”) needed to check its fingerprint. This does not reveal the secret code from the voter’s receipt. You can verify the sealed record and recount its votes without collecting anyone else’s original receipt.

Anonymous polls have a limitation: ordinary participants cannot see other people’s votes, so they cannot independently check the whole count. They can still verify their own ballots, but the seal and timestamp alone do not guarantee that the totals are correct.

Evidence you can keep

Your original receipt helps reveal a change between casting and sealing. Saved proof of the sealed record helps reveal changes made later—even by the platform operator.

You or someone you trust can use independent software to check downloaded evidence. You do not have to rely solely on a “verified” badge on our website.

Your vote stays within its access rules

The public timestamp contains only the record’s fingerprint. It does not publish your name or choices.

Poll visibility rules still apply, including authorized administrators’ access. Your receipt contains private voting information: keep the file and its code private.

A closed poll may still be waiting for its timestamp

  • Timestamp pending

    The timestamp proof is still being processed. Your receipt can be checked against an available sealed record separately; Bitcoin confirmation has not yet been established.

  • Timestamp confirmed

    The proof has verified Bitcoin evidence. It establishes that the sealed record existed by the recorded timestamp, not the exact moment you voted or the poll closed.

A poll marked unsealed has no seal proof to check. Older polls may remain unsealed.

What you should do

  • After voting: review and save the original receipt file. Keep the email attachment if that is how you received it.
  • After the poll is sealed: check your vote using that original receipt. Read the ballot-check and timestamp results separately.
  • Keep the evidence: save the downloadable record and proof files available to you, including the confirmed proof when ready.
  • If a check fails or cannot finish: keep your original files unchanged and ask the poll organizer to investigate. An incomplete check does not, by itself, establish what went wrong.

A few common questions

I lost the original receipt. Can I still check my vote?

You can check inclusion of the vote currently supplied by the platform using your receipt code. That is a weaker check: it cannot establish that the vote matches what was originally issued to you. The platform also stores the code. A fresh download does not replace an independently saved original receipt.

Does this make changing votes impossible?

It provides evidence that lets changes be detected against your original receipt and saved proof. It cannot prevent every dishonest act, such as inventing a vote before sealing for someone who never checks. Review your receipt when you receive it and verify it later.

Do I need Bitcoin or a cryptocurrency wallet?

No. Bitcoin is used as a public timestamp record. You do not need to own cryptocurrency or have a wallet to check your poll’s proof.

Does a verification link prove that a PDF has not been edited?

The link verifies the underlying voting record. It does not authenticate the exact PDF file. If you need to check a document, compare its contents with the verified record you are allowed to view.
